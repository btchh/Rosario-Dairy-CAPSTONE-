01 / THE IDEA

Forecasting is an educated estimate.

Imagine that your dairy shop writes down its revenue every month. You notice that nearby months often resemble each other, some times of year repeat, and large feeding orders behave differently from ordinary sales. A forecasting model turns those patterns into a mathematical estimate of a later total.

It does not know the future. It asks: “Given the recorded past and the patterns my model can represent, what value should I expect next?” Once the target period ends, you compare the estimate with what actually happened.

Actual

The eligible sales revenue recorded for a period. A completed month's actual is an observation. Today's running total is still partial.

Prediction / forecast

A model's estimate of a target period. It can be close or far from the outcome. The time it was made matters as much as the number.

Suppose a model estimates next month's revenue at ₱100,000, and the completed month's actual is ₱110,000. The model underpredicted by ₱10,000. These are teaching amounts, not values pulled from your business.

A time series is simply a list of observations in time order: January revenue, February revenue, March revenue, and so on. A bucket is one time container, such as a Monday–Sunday week or a calendar month. SARIMA works with a regular sequence of these buckets.

1 Record sales2 Prepare periods3 Learn patterns4 Estimate future5 Compare outcomes

Three facts to keep with you

  1. Predicted revenue is not guaranteed revenue. A nice-looking line can still be wrong.
  2. A graph is not proof of accuracy. Accuracy needs predictions paired with later, trustworthy actuals.
  3. Missing data is not automatically zero sales. An absent record might mean “not encoded yet,” not “the business earned nothing.”
Is SARIMA “AI” or a neural network?

SARIMA is a statistical time-series model. It learns numerical relationships from the sequence of past values. This implementation is not a neural network, large language model, or rule that simply increases revenue by a fixed percentage. The library performs numerical estimation of coefficients and uncertainty.

03 / THE GAP YOU ASKED ABOUT

A gap can mean several different things.

The main orange-to-green break is explained directly by your code. Past predictions are one line. Current and future predictions are another line. Neither line has both endpoints across the boundary, so the chart does not draw a connecting segment.

That is different from a vertical jump, where adjacent forecasts have different peso values, or a missing observation, where no number exists at all. Use the lab to see the distinction.

Why your orange and green lines stop at different points

'past_prediction': predicted if target < current else None,

None means there is no value for that series at that date. For September, the past-prediction field can contain a number and the future field is empty. For October, the past field is empty and the future field can contain a number.

Each Line is drawn independently. The orange line has no October point; the green line has no September point. There is no line segment spanning those two series. Changing connectNulls alone would not merge two separate lines. This guide explains the behavior; it does not change the frontend.

A vertical jump is a separate question

September's estimate may be reconstructed from records dated before September, or retained from a genuinely issued earlier forecast. October's can use the later cutoff before October. Historical reconstruction can include later ledger corrections; it does not prove that the estimate existed before September. The origin, parameters, latest observation, horizon and genuine issued-forecast provenance can differ. Forecasts are not required to “start exactly where the last prediction ended.” Forcing them to meet would alter the displayed estimates.

Four other causes of missing or separated points

  1. Unknown actual: no eligible invoices in a bucket produces a missing value, not an automatic zero.
  2. Missing component: Regular is available but Feeding is not; therefore Combined cannot be calculated.
  3. Saved graph unavailable: the exact date, source revision or engine has no matching saved graph. Retained issued predictions may still remain.
  4. Different quantities being compared: current actual is partial, while the forecast estimates the whole week, month or year.

Example: on Tuesday, ₱2,000 of recorded weekly revenue versus a ₱10,000 full-week forecast is not yet an ₱8,000 completed-week error. Most of the week has not finished. These amounts are illustrative.

Without a particular screenshot or API response, we cannot assign every gap you saw to one cause. The separate-series boundary is confirmed; the other causes should be checked using the point's dates, values, status and prediction_basis.

04 / DATA BEFORE MATHEMATICS

From an invoice to a training observation.

The model does not read a screenshot of the chart. It receives an ordered numeric series built from database records. Bad classification or missing source records can matter more than the choice of forecasting formula.

  1. Read eligible transactions. Exclude voided transactions and limit dates using an exclusive business-time cutoff.
  2. Exclude generated demonstration invoices. Reserved references matching ^2026:[0-9]{6}$ do not train the real model.
  3. Determine the sales segment. Imported source_sales_segment takes precedence. Otherwise the recorded customer name/label containing feedin, ignoring case, is a Feeding signal; other eligible sales are Regular.
  4. Apply product classification to ordinary transactions without an imported segment. The approved product policy identifies dairy and non-dairy lines. Unknown classifications, missing lines or unsuitable totals become Unclassified. Known entirely non-dairy invoices are excluded. Preclassified imported records retain their source-prepared segment and amount; they rely on the earlier import review.
  5. Use eligible revenue. The transaction's final amount is the starting revenue. For an ordinary mixed approved dairy/non-dairy invoice without an imported segment, allocate that amount by the eligible dairy share.
  6. Group by local calendar bucket. Add eligible amounts into weeks or months, keeping the segment separate.
  7. Fingerprint the source. A SHA-256 revision identifies the relevant records, policy and timezone so stale fitted results are not silently attached to different inputs.
Mixed-invoice allocation used during source preparationEligible dairy revenue = final invoice total × (dairy line amount / total line amount)

For a teaching invoice totaling ₱900 after an invoice-level discount, with ₱800 dairy and ₱200 non-dairy in the underlying line amounts, eligible dairy revenue is ₱900 × 800 / 1,000 = ₱720. That prepares the forecast target; it is not a claim that the original receipt recorded a per-line discount allocation.

Revenue, cash and demand are different

The snapshot deliberately does not filter by payment status. A recorded sale can contribute revenue even if collection is outstanding. Profit would require cost information. True demand may exceed observed sales during a stockout, so this model should not be described as measuring all customer demand.

What data can each prediction see?

  • Past weekly/monthly target: use a snapshot before that target starts.
  • Current and future weekly/monthly targets: use the current period's start as the cutoff, so the current incomplete period is excluded from training.
  • Past/current yearly target: use the start of that target year.
  • Future yearly target: use the first day of the current month. This can be a more recent origin than the current-year estimate.

For example, the current 2026 annual estimate uses data before January 1, 2026, but the future 2027 estimate as of October 27 can use data before October 1, 2026. They are not necessarily two values from one identically dated fit. If no newer actuals exist, their last observed training date can nevertheless remain the same.

Training stops earlier than displayed actuals

The centered graph can show actual sales through the selected as_of day, including today's running total. Training snapshots stop before the appropriate closed-period cutoff. This lets the blue line grow during the current period without using incomplete current sales to train its full-period prediction.

Timezone: business dates use Asia/Manila. A timestamp near midnight UTC can belong to a different local day. The code converts timestamps before assigning their calendar bucket.

06 / THE MATHEMATICS, IN SMALL STEPS

Decode SARIMA without memorizing symbols.

SARIMA stands for Seasonal AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average. Each word describes a kind of relationship that can be included in the model. Not every fitted configuration uses every part.

PartPlain meaningA useful mental picture
AutoRegressive (AR)Past values help explain the next value.Recent revenue gives information about near-future revenue.
Integrated (I)Model changes after differencing, then reconstruct the original level.Understand “how much it changed,” then add that change back.
Moving Average (MA)Past unpredicted shocks help explain current values.Correct for a recent model surprise; this is not a simple rolling average of revenue.
Seasonal (S)Include relationships separated by a repeating cycle.Monthly observations can relate to the same month 12 steps earlier.

Read the model label

The standard notationSARIMA(p, d, q)(P, D, Q)m
SymbolWhat it countsExample
pOrdinary AR lag orderp = 1 includes a one-period AR relationship.
dOrdinary differencing countd = 1 models yt − yt−1.
qOrdinary MA lag orderq = 1 includes the preceding innovation/shock term.
PSeasonal AR orderP = 1 adds an AR effect one seasonal cycle back.
DSeasonal differencing countD = 1 models a difference yt − yt−m.
QSeasonal MA orderQ = 1 adds the error effect one seasonal cycle back.
mObservations per seasonal cyclem = 12 for an annual cycle in monthly data; m = 52 is an annual approximation in weekly data.

“Seasonal” means a repeating time pattern, not necessarily weather. Selecting Weekly does not automatically mean m=7: your weekly observations use a possible annual cycle of roughly 52 weeks.

Start with the simplest useful equations

Ordinary AR(1), teaching exampleyt = c + φyt−1 + εt
yt
Actual revenue at period t.
c
A fitted intercept. It is not automatically the mean revenue.
φ
A learned coefficient controlling the relationship to the prior period.
εt
A new unexpected shock. The model cannot know this future surprise in advance.

For an ordinary stationary AR(1), the long-run mean is μ = c / (1 − φ), provided |φ| < 1. The forecast mean replaces future unknown shocks with their assumed mean of zero. That does not claim the actual shock will literally be zero.

Change the model's memory

AR(1) illustration · not your fitted coefficients

Here the previous actual is ₱120,000 and the illustrative long-run mean is ₱100,000. We set c = (1 − φ) × ₱100,000 so changing φ changes persistence without moving that mean.

ŷt+1|t = (1 − φ) × 100,000 + φ × 120,000

Larger φ holds on to the last observation more strongly. Repeated AR(1) mean forecasts tend toward the fixed mean here. This calculator does not fit a SARIMA model or reproduce your business forecasts.

Differencing: predict the change, then restore the level

Ordinary and seasonal differencesΔyt = yt − yt−1
Δmyt = yt − yt−m

If monthly revenue goes from ₱100,000 to ₱106,000, the first difference is ₱6,000. Modeling these changes can help with a drifting level. Differencing is not the same as subtracting a prior forecast. An ARIMA(0,1,0) with no drift is a random walk: its future mean can stay at the last observed level, which explains some flat beta projections.

The complete formula

General multiplicative seasonal ARIMA formφ(B)Φ(Bm)(1 − B)d(1 − Bm)D yt
= c + θ(B)Θ(Bm) εt

B is the backshift operator: Byt = yt−1, and B12yt = yt−12. It means “look this many steps back.” Lowercase polynomials describe ordinary lags; uppercase polynomials describe seasonal lags. In this convention, φ(B) = 1 − φ₁B − … − φpBp and θ(B) = 1 + θ₁B + … + θqBq. Seasonal polynomials use Bm instead of B.

You do not need to manually multiply this expression to operate the system. The SARIMAX library builds the state-space representation and estimates the unknown coefficients. The formula tells you what relationships the chosen configuration is allowed to represent. [S1]

What does multiplication of ordinary and seasonal effects do?

For SARIMA(1,0,0)(1,0,0)₁₂ with an intercept, expanding the AR side gives:

yt = c + φyt−1 + Φyt−12 − φΦyt−13 + εt

The lag-13 term comes from multiplying the two lag polynomials. It is not just “last month plus last year.” Here Φ is the seasonal coefficient and φ is the ordinary coefficient.

The configuration actually selected in the read-only check

On 27 October 2026, strict monthly run 4 selected sarima_010_100_12: SARIMA(0,1,0)(1,0,0)₁₂, with no intercept. This says seasonal AR on the once-differenced monthly series:

(1 − ΦB12)(1 − B)yt = εt
Δyt = ΦΔyt−12 + εt

In an idealized worked example, if the last monthly level is ₱50,000, the relevant prior-year monthly change is ₱4,000, and an invented fitted Φ is 0.6, the next mean is ₱50,000 + 0.6 × ₱4,000 = ₱52,400. That illustrates the formula. It is not the saved coefficient or an actual business forecast.

SARIMAX versus SARIMA: the Python class has an X because it can accept external explanatory inputs. Your current model calls do not supply exog. Weather, promotions, future prices and feeding contracts are not directly fed into this model. The library name alone does not mean those causes are included.

07 / HOW THE COMPUTER LEARNS

The formula is a shape. Fitting finds its numbers.

Picking (0,1,0)(1,0,0,12) does not tell you the seasonal coefficient Φ. The program must estimate it from the historical series. Model orders describe allowed relationships; parameters are the numerical strengths learned inside those relationships.

The strict monthly candidate list is small and fixed

Candidate keyOrdinary orderSeasonal orderIntercept

sarima_100_100_12(1, 0, 0)(1, 0, 0, 12)Yes

sarima_110_100_12(1, 1, 0)(1, 0, 0, 12)No
sarima_011_100_12(0, 1, 1)(1, 0, 0, 12)No

Every strict monthly candidate has P = 1, D = 0, Q = 0, m = 12. That is annual seasonal autoregression, not annual seasonal differencing. The engine does not try every imaginable model or automatically run an unrestricted auto_arima search.

Step 1: scale the currency values

# Exact excerpt: forecasting/seasonal.py:32–33
scale = max(float(np.mean(values)), 1.0)
history = (values / scale).tolist()

values is the array of monthly revenue totals. The code divides it by its mean, with a minimum scale of 1, so numerical estimation works with manageable magnitudes. If the mean is ₱100,000, a ₱120,000 observation becomes 1.2. Multiply the output by the same scale to return to pesos. This is not a logarithmic transform and does not change any ledger record.

Step 2: construct the state-space model

# Exact construction: forecasting/seasonal.py:23–25
return SARIMAX(history, order=tuple(spec['order']),
               seasonal_order=tuple(spec['seasonal_order']), trend=spec['trend'],
               enforce_stationarity=True, enforce_invertibility=True)
  • history supplies the observed sequence.
  • order supplies p, d and q.
  • seasonal_order supplies P, D, Q and m.
  • trend='c' includes a constant; 'n' includes no trend term.
  • enforce_stationarity constrains the AR part; differencing can still produce a nonstationary original revenue level.
  • enforce_invertibility constrains the MA part to an identifiable, stable representation.

Step 3: estimate by maximum likelihood

The library uses a Kalman filter to track an internal state and calculate one-step prediction errors as it moves through the historical sequence. An optimizer adjusts parameters to make the observed sequence more plausible under the model.

Maximum likelihood means choosing parameters that maximize the probability density assigned to the observations. It is not a promise to minimize the exact future WAPE. Parameters are fitted inside each candidate; validation then compares candidates.

Optional: what likelihood and Kalman filtering mean mathematically

A generic state-space description is xt+1 = Txt + Rηt and yt = Zxt + measurement terms. The hidden state x contains information needed to represent the lag structure. The library generates the appropriate matrices for the selected SARIMA orders.

Schematic scalar Gaussian innovation log-likelihoodlog L = −½ Σt [log(2π) + log Ft + vt2/Ft]

vt is the innovation, the difference between the next observation and its conditional prediction. Ft is its forecast variance. This schematic formula omits initialization details; statsmodels handles those. The optimizer changes the parameter vector to increase the resulting likelihood. A filter update incorporates a new observation; missing observations provide no measurement update.

The strict monthly fitter tries L-BFGS and, if needed, Powell, with at most 150 iterations per attempt. It requires convergence and finite parameters, likelihood and output. It records warnings. A failed fit is an unavailable model, not a prediction of zero revenue.

Step 4: save, then reuse the fitted state

The saved artifact contains the orders, trend, scale, normalized history, parameter vector, library version and diagnostics. A checksum helps detect an inconsistent artifact. The implementation uses structured JSON-compatible values rather than loading an arbitrary pickled Python object.

# Exact read/replay logic: forecasting/seasonal.py:70–71
filtered = _model(state['normalized_history'], state).filter(
    state['params'], cov_type='none')
return float(filtered.forecast(1)[0] * state['scale'])

.filter(saved_parameters) reconstructs the model state from the saved coefficients. It is different from .fit(), which optimizes parameters. A page GET does not start new model training.

Stationarity, ACF, PACF, AIC and BIC: do I need these?

Stationarity roughly means the modeled series has stable statistical behavior over time. Differencing can remove changes in level. ACF measures correlation at different lags; PACF studies a lag after accounting for intervening lags. They can help diagnose potential AR/MA structure.

AIC = 2k − 2 log L and BIC = k log n − 2 log L balance fit against complexity, with k parameters and n observations. They are common model-selection tools. Your primary engine instead chooses among its predefined SARIMA candidates by earlier validation WAPE. Do not claim it automatically chose orders using AIC, an ADF test, or a complete residual-diagnostic procedure.

Useful further diagnostics include residual plots, remaining autocorrelation and outliers. A convergence flag alone does not prove the assumptions are right or future predictions are accurate.

08 / TESTING WITHOUT LOOKING INTO THE FUTURE

Good evaluation respects time.

If you ask whether a model could have predicted January, it must not be trained with February's actual revenue. That would be data leakage: accidentally giving the model information that would not have existed when the prediction was needed.

Your strict monthly engine needs at least 36 contiguous completed monthly observations. At the minimum length, the first 18 months support the initial fit, the next 6 are validation targets, and the last 12 are test targets.

The model walks forward

rows[:index] means “all observations before the target index.” For July 2024, train through June. For August, train through July. For January 2025, train through December 2024. Parameters are refitted with the earlier observations at each origin.

Move the forecast origin

Calendar illustration · no fitting

Earlier test-month actuals can become training data for later one-step test forecasts once those months have finished. The model structure remains the validation-selected choice; later test outcomes do not choose a replacement winner.

What validation chooses, and what testing measures

  1. Run the fixed SARIMA candidates on the earlier validation targets.
  2. Choose the candidate with the lowest validation WAPE; predefined order breaks ties toward simpler candidates.
  3. Choose a simple baseline using validation too.
  4. Evaluate the frozen candidate choice on the later test months.
  5. Refit that selected structure on all available completed training months to produce its next estimate.

The primary selection is SARIMA-only. Simple exponential smoothing, damped Holt and ridge autoregression are comparison methods, not secret replacements for a failed SARIMA winner. If the selected candidate fails during later fitting, the engine reports a failure rather than choosing whichever candidate looked best on the test set.

Retrospective versus prospective: reconstructing forecasts now from historical records is a backtest. A genuinely prospective forecast was saved before its target outcome was known. The already-inspected 2025 data is retrospective research; rerunning it cannot create twelve months of new, untouched real-world evidence.

With six or more observed periods, the provisional engine reserves h = min(6, max(1, floor(observed_count / 5))) observed targets for validation and h for testing. It selects using the earlier validation outcomes. Seasonal AR is considered only when the initial training slice contains at least two seasonal cycles of both calendar positions and observed values: 24 months or 104 weeks.

The centered graph permits an additional sparse-data case: two to five observed periods can fit a local-level ARIMA(0,1,0) without validation. It also has an explicitly unvalidated local-level fallback when validation percentage error has a zero denominator. This means an estimate can appear even when annual seasonality has not been established.

When an exact-source saved primary monthly SARIMA is available, the Regular monthly component can reuse that checked fit. The strict primary pipeline itself does not silently fall back to an ordinary AR model. Therefore the name “SARIMA estimates” on the graph does not prove that every component point used annual seasonal terms.

Why a flat line can be a legitimate output

For a random walk with no drift, future expected changes are zero, so multiple future means can be equal. An AR model's means can also flatten as its horizon grows. Sparse Feeding invoices do not automatically reveal the next contract schedule. Flatness can be a mathematical consequence of a chosen simple model, not a broken refresh; it can still be unhelpful for planning.

Why long forecast gaps do not disappear when you refresh

If the model ends in December 2025 and you ask about October 2026, it must project the intervening steps. The code generates a path from trained_through and selects the requested targets. Those hidden intermediate predictions are not observed 2026 invoices. A ten-month-ahead estimate is not validated by a one-month-ahead backtest score.

The yearly sum

One annual target from one monthly forecast origin oŶyear|o = Σm=1…12 ŷyear,m|o

For each annual point, all twelve monthly values come from one origin's projected path. But adjacent annual points in the centered graph can use different cutoffs, as explained in chapter 05. The older chart.py yearly historical comparison instead sums twelve rolling one-month predictions with different monthly origins. That older sum is not a year-ahead forecast issued before the year started.

Why is weekly seasonality 52 instead of 7?

The input observations are already weekly totals. A lag of 7 would mean seven weeks, not seven days. The weekly research uses 52 weekly observations as an approximate year. Calendars can contain an ISO week 53; the implementation does not merge it into another week. Monthly and weekly models have distinct calendar and evidence limitations.

Learn to read an error score.

Use a completed period with both an actual and a comparable prediction. For this project, signed error is defined as prediction minus actual. Positive means overprediction; negative means underprediction.

What WAPE does not mean

WAPE of 10% means the absolute errors sum to 10% of the evaluated actual total. It does not mean every forecast is within 10%, that there is a 90% probability of success, or that the model is “90% accurate.” Large-revenue periods carry more weight. A low overall WAPE can hide serious errors in smaller periods.

If all actuals total zero, WAPE is undefined even if the predictions are zero too. The code returns None, and the frontend should show “not available,” not 0% error by assumption. MAE can still be defined. Do not substitute zero for an empty evaluation.

Compare like with like

Baseline skill on identical targets and actualsError reduction % = 100 × (1 − Σ|emodel| / Σ|ebaseline|)

Positive reduction means the model improves on the baseline; negative means it is worse. A perfectly error-free baseline makes this ratio undefined. You must compare the same segment, periods, target actuals and forecast horizon. Monthly Regular WAPE cannot be reused as Combined, Feeding, Weekly or Yearly accuracy.

Why there may be no score under the graph

The centered graph evaluates only completed past buckets where both actual and prediction exist. It keeps historical_test and issued_forecast evidence separate. The current partial bucket is excluded. With only three past points in the displayed weekly/monthly window, there may be zero usable pairs. An empty evaluation is “not yet evaluated”; one or two evaluated periods is limited evidence.

11 / HOW CERTAIN IS THE ESTIMATE?

A forecast band is not a guarantee.

A point estimate is one expected value. A band tries to describe possible error around it. Your project has more than one type of band, and they should not be called the same thing.

Strict monthly: a historical absolute-error band

The primary engine sorts the absolute errors from its test periods and selects a high order statistic:

One-based selected rank, capped at nk = min(n, ⌈0.9(n + 1)⌉),   w = k-th smallest |e|
Lower = max(0, ŷ − w),   Upper = ŷ + w

The ceiling symbol ⌈…⌉ rounds upward. With 12 monthly test points, k = min(12, 12) = 12, so w is the largest observed absolute test error. The weekly research with 13 test points also chooses the largest error under this rule.

If the largest past test error were an illustrative ₱12,000 and the prediction were ₱100,000, the displayed band would be ₱88,000–₱112,000. This summarizes historical error. It is not a validated claim that 90% of future outcomes will fall in that band.

Provisional path: nominal model-based intervals

# Exact calls: forecasting/provisional.py:172–175
result = model_for(state['values'], state['specification']).filter(
    state['parameters'])
future = result.get_forecast(steps=steps)
values = np.asarray(future.predicted_mean) * state['scale']
bounds = np.asarray(future.conf_int(alpha=.1)) * state['scale']

alpha=.1 requests a nominal 90% model interval. Under a Gaussian approximation this is often represented as ŷ ± 1.645 × forecast standard error. That statement depends on the fitted model's assumptions; it has not been calibrated to all actual business risks in your system. [S4]

The code checks finite values, clips negative point predictions to zero, keeps bounds around the point, and rounds to cents. These are practical output constraints. Rounding to ₱0.01 does not mean uncertainty is only one cent.

Yearly and Combined ranges need special care

Adding twelve monthly lower bounds and twelve upper bounds is not automatically a calibrated annual interval. Monthly errors can be correlated. Adding Regular and Feeding bounds does not calculate their joint uncertainty either.

Why dependence mattersVar(R + F) = Var(R) + Var(F) + 2 Cov(R, F)

Variance measures uncertainty; covariance describes how the two errors move together. If both segments miss high or low at the same time, their combined error differs from an independence assumption. An annual total likewise depends on the covariances between all monthly errors.

The current centered beta graph returns scalar predicted points; it does not expose those provisional lower/upper fields in its point contract. A line without a shaded band does not mean the model has no uncertainty.

“Coverage” is used in two different ways

Ledger coverage means a human reconciled the source records for a period, including whether a missing month is truly zero. Interval coverage is the share of evaluated outcomes that fall within forecast bounds. A source-coverage attestation is not evidence of 90% statistical interval coverage.

13 / WHAT “REFRESH” ACTUALLY DOES

Refreshing runs maintenance, not time travel.

Opening the graph reads saved results. A separate refresh flow queues work outside the HTTP request, so an expensive fitting process does not run while the browser waits for a normal GET.

The current endpoint accepts an empty object. The worker performs pattern investigation, strict monthly research, weekly research, provisional forecasts, centered graphs and projection-evidence maintenance. A PostgreSQL advisory lock gives one process ownership of maintenance. Fits happen outside short consistency/save locks; sources are rechecked before saving.

Same data can reuse a fit

Saved artifacts are keyed to their actual training input. Within a week or month, new current-period invoices can change displayed partial actuals without changing the closed training history. A new day's graph still needs a matching date identity, but unchanged training signatures can reuse fitted state. A period boundary or correction to training records can require new work.

The graph's as_of date is part of its identity. A new day, changed source, absent graph or failed checksum can produce refresh_required. GET deliberately avoids repairing that by training on the fly.

The midnight scheduler is separate from the web server

The provided worker/timer runs at midnight in the business timezone, normally Asia/Manila. Starting Django or deploying the web app does not install that scheduler. The supervised worker refreshes on startup to catch up today after downtime, then waits for the next run. It does not forge predictions for missed past dates.

The strict monthly issue is created only on its legitimate first-day timing when the model's next target matches today. Running a refresh on October 27 cannot create an authentic October 1 issue.

A safe learning workflow

Inspect without fitting

venv/bin/python manage.py audit_forecast_accuracy

This command reads saved results and evidence, reports accuracy and gaps, and performs no fits. Its output can contain business metrics; keep it private as appropriate.

Maintenance that writes saved model records

venv/bin/python manage.py run_forecast_refresh

This is an operator action, not part of opening this guide. Arrange and authorize it in the intended environment after reviewing the source data. It can consume CPU and save new model/evidence records.

On Windows, the virtual-environment Python path is commonly venv\Scripts\python.exe. The guide itself needs no Python command. No commands shown here are executed by this HTML file.

Prepare honestly for the academic defense

  1. Reconcile the actual ledger. Check dates, duplicates, cancellations, imported labels, product policy and segment classification with the responsible source owner.
  2. Resolve missing versus zero. Encode missing genuine records where authorized. Confirm a zero period only when source reconciliation establishes it.
  3. Provide recent completed history. For an October 2026 strict monthly forecast, that means real, usable history through September 2026, with the required continuous window.
  4. Review coverage. A source attestation belongs to a particular month and source revision. Correcting those records can invalidate the earlier review.
  5. Run authorized offline maintenance. Read status and gate reasons afterwards. A successful worker job does not promise publication.
  6. Distinguish research from live evidence. Show retrospective results as research, explain the simple baseline, and state the prospective evidence still missing.

Do not fill unexplained months with zero, copy old predictions into “actual,” backdate issue records, generate demo invoices as real history, or change thresholds just to get a green status. These actions would remove the meaning of the evaluation.

15 / SPEAK ABOUT IT CLEARLY

Questions you may be asked.

Use these as explanations to understand, not statements to memorize without checking the current evidence.

“What does your forecasting feature predict?”

It estimates eligible dairy-sales revenue in Philippine pesos. The dashboard separates Regular, Feeding and their Combined total. The strict approved-model lifecycle evaluates one-month-ahead Regular revenue. It does not establish accuracy for liters, stock, profit or every displayed horizon.

“Why SARIMA?”

Revenue is time-ordered and can have recent dependence and recurring calendar patterns. SARIMA can represent those relationships through ordinary and seasonal AR/MA terms and differencing. The implementation compares fixed candidates and simple baselines. Suitability still depends on data and measured evidence; selecting the model family alone does not prove it is best.

“What is the formula in your implementation?”

The general family is SARIMA(p,d,q)(P,D,Q)ₘ. The checked strict model chose (0,1,0)(1,0,0)₁₂: monthly changes are related to changes twelve months earlier. The model estimates the seasonal coefficient from historical data. That was run 4 on the dated read-only check; future model selection can differ.

“Why is there a break from last month's prediction to this month's?”

The current dashboard deliberately places past estimates and current/future estimates in separate chart series. There is no connecting segment across them. A difference in their heights can additionally reflect a new origin or fit. Missing values, absent saved work and partial current actuals are different cases that must be checked separately.

“Why can the forecast be higher than today's recorded amount?”

The forecast is a full-period estimate, while today's actual is only the running total. A current week, month or year is incomplete. Accuracy should be evaluated after the period closes and its source records are reviewed.

“Your WAPE is 10.64%; is the system 89.36% accurate?”

No. The historical test's absolute errors total about 10.64% of the actual revenue total for that evaluated scope. It is not a per-prediction success probability or a universal accuracy percentage. The 2025 result is retrospective research, and it must be compared with the same-target baseline and prospective evidence.

“Why is the status stale_data if the graph shows forecasts?”

The graph and the strict publication endpoint are separate. A saved exploratory graph can be available while the strict one-month model ends too far in the past. On the dated check, genuine eligible sales ended December 2025, the strict next target was behind October 2026, and reviewed/prospective publication evidence was absent.

“Did the model train on the future while testing?”

The walk-forward loop fits only the rows before each target. Earlier target outcomes can join later training once they are in the past. Candidate choice is frozen from the earlier validation window; later test results do not select a replacement winner. Reconstructed tests still use corrected historical ledger records, so they are not proof of the information actually available at the old date.

“Does a yearly point use a model trained on annual totals?”

The centered graph's annual point sums twelve monthly forecasts from one origin. Adjacent annual points can use different origin cutoffs. The older chart's yearly historical sum uses rolling monthly predictions and is not the same as an annual forecast issued before January.

“Why is Feeding sometimes flat or unavailable?”

Feeding sales may be sparse and depend on schedules not provided as external inputs. A simple local-level model can produce flat mean forecasts; too little usable history can withhold a prediction. A plotted estimate is not a validated contract schedule.

“What happens if records are corrected?”

The source fingerprint changes. A saved fit or coverage attestation for older records may no longer match. New maintenance can save a new version, while original issued forecasts and revision-specific scores are retained. This makes the provenance inspectable instead of rewriting old predictions.

“What still needs to happen before relying on this operationally?”

Reconcile source completeness and classification, supply recent genuine data, review coverage, run appropriate maintenance and collect current prospective issued-forecast evidence. Assess the actual error and baseline for the intended horizon and segment. Configure and verify the deployment scheduler and monitoring. The existing gates should report the missing evidence honestly.
